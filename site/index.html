Open source daily planner

Four tasks. No fifth.

Doer is built on one rule. Pick at most four tasks that count toward the day, and the day is kept if you finish at least 75% of them. Four tasks means three, three means three, two means both. Everything else in the application exists to protect that rule.

Star the repo How it works

Self-hosted. It runs as a Docker Compose stack wherever you put it, on your own machine or your own server, against your own Postgres. Clone it and the README takes you from there.

  • The capFour primary tasks per day. The fifth is refused, with a remedy attached: demote an existing primary, or add this one as non-primary.
  • The verdictSeventy five percent of the primaries completed keeps the day, so four tasks means three, three means three, and two means both. Two of four does not pass.
  • The neutral floorFewer than two primaries leaves the day unscoreable. A one-task day is not a win and it is not a loss, so it is neither.
  • Carry forwardUnfinished work rolls to the next day and keeps its slot. Tasks you had pre-assigned there give way, because a commitment outranks an intention.
  • PermanenceA completed task cannot be edited or deleted. Deletion is refused twice, once by the service and once by a database trigger that also closes the cascade and bulk paths a service check cannot reach. Completions can be reversed, but only before the day closes.

The logical day

Your day is not the calendar's day.

You say when it starts and how long it runs. A day from 14:00 to 05:00 is one day, so a task finished at 03:00 counts toward the day that began yesterday afternoon, not toward tomorrow.

Frozen at open

A day's shape is stored, not derived. Change your hours tomorrow and yesterday keeps the shape it was measured against. A day that has been scored cannot be rewritten after the fact.

Real hours, not nominal ones

A fifteen hour day across a spring forward transition is fourteen real hours, and the capacity figures follow, because you genuinely have one hour less.

Closing is lazy

Nothing closes your day on a timer. The next time you open the app it closes every elapsed day in order, scores them, and carries the unfinished work forward. Shut it for a week and it is still correct.

Over-commitment

Shown, not shouted.

Doer estimates how long each task takes, sums the open ones, and compares that against the time you actually have: your focus blocks, minus the busy time on your calendar, times a ratio that defaults to half. Go over, and a small orange dot appears next to the date.

That is the entire warning. No modal, no notification, no streak to protect, no red banner telling you that you are behind.

It clears itself

The dot tracks the live figure, so finishing work makes it go away. The latched version of the flag is kept separately, for the metrics, so that a day which was over-committed all morning does not record itself as fine.

Unknown never reads as headroom

No focus blocks defined, or a task Doer has not estimated yet, makes the day indeterminate rather than safe. It says it does not know, instead of implying you have room.

The surface

Three screens and a detail view.

One navigation bar, no settings maze, no dashboard of widgets.

/todayToday

The four-slot header, your tasks grouped by block, completed work below. Add by typing a task, or by telling the assistant what your day looks like. Reorder by dragging.

/historyHistory

Current streak, days kept, and a rolling seven day rate, over a heatmap of the last six months. Two views: how much you finished, and whether the day was kept.

/history/:anchorDay detail

Any cell opens the day behind it: the outcome it was given, and the tasks it was given it for.

/settingsSettings

The day's shape and your focus blocks, edited by dragging directly on a timeline. Drag the window to move it, drag an edge to resize, or tab to it and use the arrow keys. It cannot produce an overlap.

The assistant

It proposes. You commit.

Tell it what your day looks like in a sentence and it returns a proposal: tasks, blocks, estimates. Nothing is written until you confirm it, and you can edit it first. It does not decompose your work into subtasks, it does not reorganise your day behind your back, and it never adds a fifth primary.

Estimation runs separately on a smaller model, in fifteen minute buckets, retried in the background when it fails and cached so the same task is not priced twice. Both run on your own Anthropic API key.

Four tasks is a small promise.

Keeping it most days is not. Star the repo if the idea is worth trying, and clone it if you want to run it tonight. The README has the setup.
